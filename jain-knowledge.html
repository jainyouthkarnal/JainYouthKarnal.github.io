FIVE AUSPICIOUS MILESTONES

The Five Kalyanaks (पंच कल्याणक)

In Jain philosophy, the life of each Tirthankara is marked by five auspicious events known as Panch Kalyanaka. These events signify profound stages of soul purification and are commemorated through worship, celebration, and inner contemplation.

01 • Conception

Garbh Kalyanaka (गर्भ कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The auspicious conception when the pure soul of a future Tirthankara descends into the mother's womb for its final human incarnation before liberation.

Spiritual significance: Reminds devotees that the spiritual path begins with cultivating noble intention, pure thoughts, and auspicious virtue.

Reflection: How can I orient my thoughts and actions toward purposeful spiritual growth?

02 • Birth

Janma Kalyanaka (जन्म कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The birth of the Tirthankara. Celebrated by celestial beings on Mount Meru with ceremonial Janmabhishek.

Spiritual significance: Celebrates the arrival of a divine beacon who will guide all living beings toward freedom from suffering.

Reflection: How can I dedicate my human birth to practising non-violence (Ahimsa) and compassion?

03 • Renunciation

Tapa / Diksha Kalyanaka (तप / दीक्षा कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The renunciation of worldly comforts, wealth, and status to adopt the monastic path of pure asceticism.

Spiritual significance: Highlights that supreme peace is achieved through detachment (Vairagya), self-discipline, and inner contemplation.

Reflection: Which material attachment or habit can I renounce today to cultivate mindful living?

04 • Omniscience

Kevalgyan Kalyanaka (केवलज्ञान कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The destruction of all four destructive (Ghatiya) karmas and the attainment of infinite, unobstructed knowledge.

Spiritual significance: Marks the delivery of the divine sermon (Divya Dhwani) in the Samavasharana, expounding the eternal path of liberation.

Reflection: Am I actively studying right knowledge (Samyak Jnana) and acting in alignment with truth?

05 • Liberation

Moksha / Nirvana Kalyanaka (मोक्ष / निर्वाण कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The shedding of all remaining karmic bonds and attainment of eternal liberation in the realm of Siddhas.

Spiritual significance: The ultimate goal of Jain Dharma: eternal bliss, pure consciousness, and complete cessation of the cycle of rebirth.

Reflection: What steps am I taking daily to reduce anger, ego, deceit, and greed?

Core Takeaway

Path of Spiritual Ascension

The Kalyanaks provide a practical roadmap for every spiritual aspirant:

• Garbh: Pure aspiration.
• Janma: Compassionate living.
• Tapa: Voluntary restraint.
• Kevalgyan: Enlightened understanding.
• Moksha: Complete liberation.

CELIBACY & PURITY

Panch Balayati Tirthankaras (पंच बालयति तीर्थंकर)

In Digambara tradition, five Tirthankaras observed lifelong celibacy from childhood, renounced worldly reign, and attained supreme asceticism without entering family life:

  • 12. Shri Vasupujya Swami (श्री वासुपूज्य स्वामी)
  • 19. Shri Mallinath Swami (श्री मल्लिनाथ स्वामी)
  • 22. Shri Neminath Swami (श्री नेमिनाथ स्वामी)
  • 23. Shri Parshvanath Swami (श्री पार्श्वनाथ स्वामी)
  • 24. Shri Mahavir Swami (श्री महावीर स्वामी)

Traditional Panch Balayati Verse

सजि वसु-विधि द्रव्य मनोज्ञ, अरघ बनावत हैं।
वसु-कर्म अनादि-संयोग, ताहि नशावत हैं॥
श्री वासुपूज्य, मल्लि, नेमि, पारस, वीर अती।
मन-वच-तन धरि प्रेम, पूजों पंच बालयती॥

Dedication Mantra

ॐ ह्रीं श्री वासुपूज्य-मल्लिनाथ-नेमिनाथ-पार्श्वनाथ-महावीरस्वामी पंच-बालयति-तीर्थंकरेभ्यो अनर्घ्यपदप्राप्तये अर्घ्यं निर्वपामीति स्वाहा।

TEN SUPREME VIRTUES

Das Lakshan Parva (दशलक्षण पर्व)

Observed annually during the month of Bhadrapada (Shukla Panchami to Chaturdashi), this ten-day festival is dedicated to spiritual purification through the contemplation and practice of ten supreme virtues (Uttam Dharmas):

Day 01

Uttam Kshama (उत्तम क्षमा)

Supreme Forgiveness: Overcoming anger (krodh) and showing unconditional forgiveness to all living beings.

Day 02

Uttam Mardava (उत्तम मार्दव)

Supreme Modesty & Humility: Completely relinquishing arrogance, ego, and pride of caste, power, knowledge, or wealth (maan).

Day 03

Uttam Aarjava (उत्तम आर्जव)

Supreme Straightforwardness: Living with upright honesty and total transparency without deceit or trickery (maya).

Day 04

Uttam Shauch (उत्तम शौच)

Supreme Contentment & Purity: Cleansing internal desires by renouncing greed (lobh) and cultivating contentment.

Day 05

Uttam Satya (उत्तम सत्य)

Supreme Truthfulness: Speaking beneficial, measured, and harmless truth that upholds universal compassion.

Day 06

Uttam Sanyam (उत्तम संयम)

Supreme Self-Restraint: Protecting all life forms (Prani-Sanyam) and exercising discipline over the senses (Indriya-Sanyam).

Day 07

Uttam Tapa (उत्तम तप)

Supreme Austerity: Practising internal and external penances to burn away past accumulated karmas.

Day 08

Uttam Tyaga (उत्तम त्याग)

Supreme Renunciation: Practising fourfold charity (food, medicine, knowledge, protection) with a selfless heart.

Day 09

Uttam Akinchanya (उत्तम आकिंचन्य)

Supreme Non-Attachment: Cultivating internal detachment from possessions and recognising that the soul owns nothing material.

Day 10

Uttam Brahmacharya (उत्तम ब्रह्मचर्य)

Supreme Celibacy & Soul Realization: Attuning the consciousness exclusively to the divine nature of the inner soul.

Kshamavani Parva (क्षमावाणी)

Observed immediately following Das Lakshan Parva, Kshamavani is the universal day of forgiveness where every Jain seeks and grants heartfelt pardon:

खम्मामि सव्वजीवेसु, सव्वे जीवा खमंतु मे।
मित्ती मे सव्वभूएस्, वेरं मज्झं न केणइ॥

"I forgive all living beings; may all souls forgive me. I have friendship with all; malice toward none." — Michchhami Dukkadam.
