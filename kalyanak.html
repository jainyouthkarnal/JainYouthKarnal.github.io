FIVE AUSPICIOUS MILESTONES

The Five Kalyanakas (पंच कल्याणक)

In Jain tradition, the life of each Tirthankara is associated with five auspicious events known as Panch Kalyanaka. These events mark important spiritual milestones and are commemorated through worship, reflection, and religious ceremonies. Dates, places, and details vary by Tirthankara and traditional account.

01 · Conception

Garbh Kalyanaka (गर्भ कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The auspicious conception of the future Tirthankara, when the soul takes birth in the mother’s womb for its final human life before liberation.

Spiritual significance: It reminds devotees that a Tirthankara’s final birth is connected with an extraordinary spiritual journey toward the welfare and guidance of all living beings.

Traditional observances: Worship, devotional readings, and ceremonies recalling the auspicious dreams traditionally associated with the mother.

Reflection: How can I make my own life more purposeful and oriented toward spiritual growth?

02 · Birth

Janma Kalyanaka (जन्म कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The birth of the Tirthankara.

Spiritual significance: It celebrates the appearance in the world of a soul that will eventually attain omniscience and show the path to liberation.

Traditional observances: Celebrations may include Janmabhishek (ceremonial bathing of the Tirthankara’s image), processions, worship, and recitation of devotional texts. Practices differ among communities.

Reflection: How can I use my life to practise non-violence, self-restraint, and compassion?

03 · Renunciation

Tapa or Diksha Kalyanaka (तप / दीक्षा कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The Tirthankara’s renunciation of worldly life and initiation into the ascetic path.

Spiritual significance: It represents turning away from attachment to possessions, status, and sensory pleasures in pursuit of self-discipline and liberation.

Traditional observances: Worship, scriptural study, and reflection on restraint, simplicity, and non-attachment.

Reflection: Which attachment or habit can I reduce to live with greater awareness and self-control?

04 · Omniscience

Kevalgyan Kalyanaka (केवलज्ञान कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The attainment of Keval Jnana, perfect and unobstructed knowledge.

Spiritual significance: In Jain philosophy, Keval Jnana is complete knowledge attained when the soul’s knowledge-obscuring karmas have been destroyed. It marks the Tirthankara’s omniscience and teaching of the path to liberation.

Traditional observances: Worship, scriptural recitation, and teachings about right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct.

Reflection: How can I strengthen right understanding and make my conduct consistent with what I know to be right?

05 · Liberation

Moksha or Nirvana Kalyanaka (मोक्ष / निर्वाण कल्याणक)

What it commemorates: The Tirthankara’s liberation from the cycle of birth and death.

Spiritual significance: In Jain belief, the liberated soul is free from all karmic bondage and does not take another birth. This is the culmination of the spiritual path.

Traditional observances: Remembering sacred Nirvana Kshetras, offering worship, studying accounts of liberation, and reflecting on the goal of moksha.

Reflection: What can I do today to lessen anger, pride, deceit, greed, and attachment?

Why they matter

A framework for spiritual reflection

  • Garbh: Recognise the potential for spiritual progress.
  • Janma: Celebrate the opportunity to practise dharma.
  • Diksha: Learn from renunciation and self-restraint.
  • Kevalgyan: Aspire to right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct.
  • Moksha: Keep liberation as the ultimate spiritual goal.

Ceremonies, narratives, and the exact chronology associated with each Kalyanaka can vary across Jain traditions and individual Tirthankara accounts.

DEVOTIONAL LEARNING

Panch Balayati Tirthankaras (पंच बालयति)

The five Balayati Tirthankaras are traditionally named as follows:

  1. Shri Vasupujya Swami
    श्री वासुपूज्यनाथ भगवान
  2. Shri Mallinath Swami
    श्री मल्लिनाथ भगवान
  3. Shri Neminath Swami
    श्री नेमिनाथ भगवान
  4. Shri Parshvanath Swami
    श्री पार्श्वनाथ भगवान
  5. Shri Mahavir Swami
    श्री महावीर स्वामी भगवान

Traditional Panch Balayati verse

श्री वासुपूज्य मल्लि नेमि, पारस वीर अती।

नमूँ मन-वच-तन धरि प्रेम, पाँचों बालयती॥

Associated mantra

ॐ ह्रीं श्री वासुपूज्य-मल्लिनाथ-नेमिनाथ-पार्श्वनाथ-महावीरस्वामी पंचबालयति-तीर्थंकरेभ्यो अनर्घ्यपद-प्राप्तये अर्घ्यं निर्वपामीति स्वाहा।

TEN SUPREME VIRTUES

Das Lakshan Parva (दशलक्षण पर्व)

Das Lakshan Parva is a ten-day sacred festival observed by Digambara Jains to focus on soul purification, spiritual growth, and the practice of ten supreme virtues (Uttam Dharmas). It is observed from Bhadrapada Shukla Panchami to Bhadrapada Shukla Chaturdashi, with one virtue contemplated each day through worship, scriptural study, and austerity.

The 10 Days and Virtues

Day 1

Uttam Kshama (उत्तम क्षमा)

Supreme Forgiveness — practising tolerance and completely letting go of anger (krodh).

Day 2

Uttam Mardava (उत्तम मार्दव)

Supreme Modesty / Humility — overcoming pride, vanity, and ego (man).

Day 3

Uttam Aarjava (उत्तम आर्जव)

Supreme Straightforwardness / Honesty — living with deceit-free, upright conduct (maya).

Day 4

Uttam Shauch (उत्तम शौच)

Supreme Contentment / Purity — keeping the mind, body, and speech pure by discarding greed (lobh).

Day 5

Uttam Satya (उत्तम सत्य)

Supreme Truthfulness — speaking kind, true, and beneficial words that cause no harm to any living being.

Day 6

Uttam Sanyam (उत्तम संयम)

Supreme Self-Restraint — protecting all life forms and exercising restraint over the five senses and the mind.

Day 7

Uttam Tap (उत्तम तप)

Supreme Austerity / Penance — practising self-discipline, fasting, and checking worldly desires.

Day 8

Uttam Tyag (उत्तम त्याग)

Supreme Renunciation — giving fourfold charity (food, fearlessness, medicine, and scriptural knowledge) and supporting religious causes.

Day 9

Uttam Akinchanya (उत्तम आकिंचन्य)

Supreme Non-Attachment — cultivating detachment from material wealth and internal passions.

Day 10

Uttam Brahmacharya (उत्तम ब्रह्मचर्य)

Supreme Celibacy / Chastity — devoting oneself to the purity of the inner soul and discarding carnal and worldly desires.

Key Practices

Jains may engage in temple worship, scriptural study, meditation, and fasting, ranging from partial diets to complete fasts.

Anant Chaturdashi

The tenth day coincides with Anant Chaturdashi, which marks the Nirvana of Lord Vasupujya.

Kshamavani

Observed a day after the festival concludes, Kshamavani is a time to ask for and grant forgiveness (Michchhami Dukkadam) to family, friends, and all living beings for any hurt caused over the past year.
