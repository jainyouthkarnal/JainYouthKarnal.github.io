# Panch Balayati and Das Lakshan Parva

## Panch Balayati Tirthankaras

The five Balayati Tirthankaras are traditionally named as:

1. **Shri Vasupujya Swami** — श्री वासुपूज्यनाथ भगवान
2. **Shri Mallinath Swami** — श्री मल्लिनाथ भगवान
3. **Shri Neminath Swami** — श्री नेमिनाथ भगवान
4. **Shri Parshvanath Swami** — श्री पार्श्वनाथ भगवान
5. **Shri Mahavir Swami** — श्री महावीर स्वामी भगवान

Traditional Panch Balayati verse:

> श्री वासुपूज्य मल्लि नेमि, पारस वीर अती।  
> नमूँ मन-वच-तन धरि प्रेम, पाँचों बालयती॥

Associated mantra:

> ॐ ह्रीं श्री वासुपूज्य-मल्लिनाथ-नेमिनाथ-पार्श्वनाथ-महावीरस्वामी पंचबालयति-तीर्थंकरेभ्यो अनर्घ्यपद-प्राप्तये अर्घ्यं निर्वपामीति स्वाहा।

## The Five Kalyanakas (पंच कल्याणक)

In Jain tradition, the life of each Tirthankara is associated with five auspicious events known as *Panch Kalyanaka*. These events mark important spiritual milestones in the Tirthankara’s life and are commemorated through worship, reflection, and religious ceremonies. The dates, places, and details vary by Tirthankara and by traditional account.

### 1. Garbh Kalyanaka (गर्भ कल्याणक) — Conception

- **What it commemorates:** The auspicious conception of the future Tirthankara, when the soul takes birth in the mother’s womb for its final human life before liberation.
- **Spiritual significance:** It reminds devotees that a Tirthankara’s final birth is connected with an extraordinary spiritual journey toward the welfare and guidance of all living beings.
- **Traditional observances:** Communities may mark the occasion with worship, devotional readings, and ceremonies that recall the auspicious dreams traditionally associated with the mother.
- **Reflection:** How can I make my own life more purposeful and oriented toward spiritual growth?

### 2. Janma Kalyanaka (जन्म कल्याणक) — Birth

- **What it commemorates:** The birth of the Tirthankara.
- **Spiritual significance:** It celebrates the appearance in the world of a soul that will eventually attain omniscience and show the path to liberation.
- **Traditional observances:** Celebrations may include *Janmabhishek* (ceremonial bathing of the Tirthankara’s image), processions, worship, and recitation of devotional texts. Practices differ among communities.
- **Reflection:** How can I use my life to practise non-violence, self-restraint, and compassion?

### 3. Tapa or Diksha Kalyanaka (तप / दीक्षा कल्याणक) — Renunciation and Initiation

- **What it commemorates:** The Tirthankara’s renunciation of worldly life and initiation into the ascetic path.
- **Spiritual significance:** It represents turning away from attachment to possessions, status, and sensory pleasures in pursuit of self-discipline and liberation.
- **Traditional observances:** Devotees may recall the renunciation through worship, scriptural study, and reflection on restraint, simplicity, and non-attachment.
- **Reflection:** Which attachment or habit can I reduce to live with greater awareness and self-control?

### 4. Kevalgyan Kalyanaka (केवलज्ञान कल्याणक) — Attainment of Omniscience

- **What it commemorates:** The attainment of *Keval Jnana*, perfect and unobstructed knowledge.
- **Spiritual significance:** In Jain philosophy, Keval Jnana is the complete knowledge attained when the soul’s knowledge-obscuring karmas have been destroyed. It marks the Tirthankara’s omniscience and the teaching of the path to liberation.
- **Traditional observances:** Worship, scriptural recitation, and teachings about right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct may form part of the commemoration.
- **Reflection:** How can I strengthen right understanding and make my conduct consistent with what I know to be right?

### 5. Moksha or Nirvana Kalyanaka (मोक्ष / निर्वाण कल्याणक) — Liberation

- **What it commemorates:** The Tirthankara’s liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
- **Spiritual significance:** In Jain belief, the liberated soul is free from all karmic bondage and does not take another birth. This is the culmination of the spiritual path.
- **Traditional observances:** Devotees may visit or remember sacred *Nirvana Kshetras*, offer worship, study accounts of the Tirthankara’s liberation, and reflect on the goal of moksha.
- **Reflection:** What can I do today to lessen anger, pride, deceit, greed, and attachment?

### Why the Five Kalyanakas Matter

The Panch Kalyanakas are not only milestones in the lives of the Tirthankaras; they also offer devotees a framework for spiritual reflection:

- **Garbh:** Recognise the potential for spiritual progress.
- **Janma:** Celebrate the opportunity to practise dharma.
- **Diksha:** Learn from renunciation and self-restraint.
- **Kevalgyan:** Aspire to right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct.
- **Moksha:** Keep liberation as the ultimate spiritual goal.

*Note:* Ceremonies, narratives, and the exact chronology associated with each Kalyanaka can vary across Jain traditions and individual Tirthankara accounts.

## Das Lakshan Parva

Das Lakshan Parva is the ten-day Digambar Jain festival of the ten supreme virtues of the soul, observed from Bhadrapada Shukla Panchami to Bhadrapada Shukla Chaturdashi, when one dharma such as forgiveness, humility or truth is contemplated each day through worship, scriptural study and austerity.

Das Lakshan Parva is a 10-day sacred festival celebrated by Digambara Jains to focus on soul purification, spiritual growth, and the practice of ten supreme virtues (Uttam Dharmas).

## The 10 Days and Virtues

Each day of the festival is dedicated to contemplating and practicing one specific virtue:

- **Day 1: Uttam Kshama (Supreme Forgiveness)** — Practicing tolerance and completely letting go of anger (*krodh*).
- **Day 2: Uttam Mardava (Supreme Modesty/Humility)** — Overcoming pride, vanity, and ego (*man*).
- **Day 3: Uttam Aarjava (Supreme Straightforwardness/Honesty)** — Living with a deceit-free, upright conduct (*maya*).
- **Day 4: Uttam Shauch (Supreme Contentment/Purity)** — Keeping the mind, body, and speech pure by discarding greed (*lobh*).
- **Day 5: Uttam Satya (Supreme Truthfulness)** — Speaking kind, true, and beneficial words that cause no harm to any living being.
- **Day 6: Uttam Sanyam (Supreme Self-Restraint)** — Protecting all life forms and exercising restraint over the five senses and the mind.
- **Day 7: Uttam Tap (Supreme Austerity/Penance)** — Practicing self-discipline, fasting, and putting a check on worldly desires.
- **Day 8: Uttam Tyag (Supreme Renunciation)** — Giving fourfold charity (food, fearlessness, medicine, and scriptural knowledge) and supporting religious causes.
- **Day 9: Uttam Akinchanya (Supreme Non-Attachment)** — Cultivating detachment from material wealth and internal passions.
- **Day 10: Uttam Brahmacharya (Supreme Celibacy/Chastity)** — Devoting oneself to the purity of the inner soul and discarding carnal and worldly desires.

## Key Practices & Conclusion

- **Rituals:** Jains engage in temple worship, scriptural study, meditation, and fasting (from partial diets to complete fasts).
- **Anant Chaturdashi:** The 10th day coincides with Anant Chaturdashi, marking the Nirvana of Lord Vasupujya.
- **Kshamavani:** Observed a day after the festival concludes, where devotees ask for and grant forgiveness (*Michchhami Dukkadam*) to family, friends, and all living beings for any hurt caused over the past year.
